# P7 - Subject expert: urban innovation and education sciences

Owns: relevance. Ran T01 and T07, judging the twenty-item sample in `MEASURE.md` section 3 against the
five live projects.

## Verdict

I judged all twenty items. **Eighteen of twenty are on the reader's patch**, and several are strong: the
epistemic-agency clarification, the complexity reframing of the curriculum, the co-production versus PAR
piece, and the teachers-and-GenAI study are all things I would put in front of this reader myself. Two
are clear false positives, and they are of two different kinds, which matters. One is a **correction
notice** ("Correction: Epistemic laundering...") filed as if it were the paper: that is a structural
class and it will recur, because Crossref lists corrections with the same type as articles. The other is
a **Dutch lobby-news item** at score 2 that the collection's own floor excludes, so it should never have
entered. A 10% false-positive rate on a sample of twenty is not alarming for a personal watch, but both
kinds are filterable, and a reader who finds a correction in the queue twice will stop trusting it.

A second, quieter problem: the reason field carries the weight of the whole instrument, and its quality
is uneven across runs. The 28 items of 12 October have specific, arguable reasons. Two legacy items have
a six-word reason ("Teacher education quality debate."), which tells the reader nothing he did not read
in the title. The instrument is only as good as that sentence.

## What would change my mind

Findings 1 and 2 need two small filters, and finding 3 needs one rule in the judgement pass. If those
are in place I would raise my verdict on the collection to "trustworthy for a working reader". I would
not ask for more sources or a bigger corpus: this reader needs a smaller, sharper set, not a wider one.
