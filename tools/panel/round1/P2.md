# P2 - Information-retrieval researcher

Owns: ranking, recall, precision, duplicates. Ran T02, T05, T06, T13 against the frozen build and the
measurement in `MEASURE.md`.

## Verdict

The pipeline is honest and the mechanics are sound, but two numbers do not support the promise of the
radar. **The harvest saw 115 of the 395 papers that the watched journals published in the window (29%),
and the collection kept 16 of them (14% of what it saw, 4% of what was published).** Most of that loss
is not a filtering decision: it is a query size. The watch asked Crossref for 10 rows per journal while
the journals published up to 60 in the same week. A watch that sees under a third of its own reading
list is not a watch, it is a sample. Separately, the discourse id rule is not deterministic, and the
collection holds the same ScienceGuide article twice. The scoring is defensible; the retrieval is not.

## Findings

| # | Sev | Feature | Finding | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S1 | F13 | Recall of the harvest is 29% (115 of 395). The cause is `--limit 10` per journal, which truncates the query before relevance is considered. | `MEASURE.md` section 1: per journal, published in the window versus seen. `harvest.py` passes `rows=limit` (10 in the catch-up run). | Raise the journal rows to 60 (Crossref's page size) and paginate with `offset` when a journal has more. Record the truncation per journal in `lastRun`. |
| 2 | S1 | F06 | The kept share of 14% is not evidence of strictness: it is the effect of a thin shortlist. A filter cannot recover a paper the query never returned. | 115 seen, 16 kept; 99 dropped by score, exclusion or the 30-item cap. | Separate the two losses in the report: "not returned by the query" and "returned but not kept". They need different fixes. |
| 3 | S1 | F04 | The discourse id rule is not deterministic, so one article entered twice. | `MEASURE.md` section 2: `sg-geveke-ai-cursus` and `sg-hva-bestuurder-henk-geveke-wil-verplichte-ai-c` share one URL and one title. The second id is the URL tail truncated at 46 characters. | Normalise the URL (strip the scheme, the trailing slash and the query), hash it, and use `sg-<hash8>`; keep a title hash as a second guard. Merge the existing pair. |
| 4 | S2 | F07 | There is no order that answers "what is new since I last looked" against "what is strongest for my current paper". `run` and `score` are separate orders, and the filter cannot combine them. | `fSort` offers score, date, run and title; `state` keeps one value at a time. | Compound orders, for example "new in this run, strongest first". |
| 5 | S2 | F12 | No relevance feedback reaches the ranker. The radar cannot learn that the reader wants fewer Dutch policy items, which is exactly the signal the kit describes. | No triage path (P1, finding 1); no scoring weight in the profile. | Use the triage as the weight source: report per theme and per source what the reader kept against what he skipped. |
| 6 | S3 | F05 | The theme filter is single-valued, and 41 items carry one theme only, which suggests under-tagging rather than a clean theme. | Data audit: 41 of 162 items have a single theme. | Allow a multi-select filter, and let the judgement pass add a second theme where it is earned. |
| 7 | S3 | F06 | The relevance sample holds a correction notice as if it were a paper ("Correction: Epistemic laundering..."). | `MEASURE.md` section 3, item 10 of 20. | Drop titles that begin with Correction, Erratum, Retraction or Author Correction before scoring. |

## What would change my mind

Findings 1 and 2 are one change each: raise the rows, and report the two losses apart. If the harvest
recall reaches the high nineties with pagination, the rest of the machinery is worth keeping. I would
not accept any redesign that ignores the query size: no interface can fix a query that never ran.
