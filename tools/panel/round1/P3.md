# P3 - Research librarian and review methodologist

Owns: coverage, the journal watch, citable metadata, provenance. Ran T05 and T14.

## Verdict

The evidence base is better than the instrument that presents it. The journal list is well chosen and
the switch-on discipline is documented per title, which is rare and good. But three things would fail a
librarian's review. First, the watch is not complete: 20 of 20 titles were swept, yet 280 of 395 papers
in the window were never returned, so a systematic search on this data would not be reproducible.
Second, the collection breaks its own written policy: four items sit below `minScore`, fifteen items
have no caveat, and a correction notice is filed as a paper. Third, the reader cannot see where a record
came from: which source, whether the abstract was read, and how exact the date is. For citation work
that last point is decisive, not cosmetic.

Empty but declared is worse than absent. |

## What would change my mind

Finding 1 is the one I would insist on: without provenance, no reader can cite from this radar with
confidence, and the radar's own `why` text is the thing being trusted. Findings 2 and 3 are honesty
problems, and honesty is cheap to repair: report the two coverage numbers side by side, and let the
validator refuse a record that breaks the policy.
