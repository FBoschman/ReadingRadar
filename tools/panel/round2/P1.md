# P1 - The owner (round 2, on the R1 build)

## Verdict

It is a different instrument. I can open it on my phone, I can search the abstracts, I can see why a reason exists and where it came from, I can mark items keep or skip, and I can see whether the intake note for an item is in my vault. The Monday review is now possible. Two things are still not what the brief promises. The candidates' queue is readable but I cannot turn a candidate into an item from here, so the queue and the collection are two worlds with a command line between them. And the radar still does not *use* what I mark: it counts my skips by theme and by source, and then scores the next run exactly as before. The learning is on the page, not in the machine.

## Findings

| # | Sev | Feature | Finding | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S2 | F12 | My decisions are counted but not used. The calibration card names a source that earns no keep, and nothing follows from it. | `renderCalibration` computes the counts; no weight, exclusion or score depends on them. | Let the reader accept a proposal in the card; write it into the profile as a weight or an exclusion, and show the effect in the next run's counts. |
| 2 | S2 | F23 | I can read and mark the queue, but a candidate I like still needs `promote.py`, a judgement file and a score. The queue is where the work is. | The Queue view has marks; promotion is a separate tool. | Offer a "promote from here" path: mark a candidate keep, with a one-line reason, and let one command write the item. |
| 3 | S3 | F09 | Where no abstract exists (about a third of the collection) the reason rests on a title, and the record says so honestly. That is right, but those items feel thin next to the rest. | 105 of 160 items carry an abstract. | Nothing to change; keep the honesty. Consider dropping an item whose only evidence is a title. |
| 4 | S3 | F02 | The digest now lists the items it names, which is what I asked for. The prose still does not link in place. | `related` renders as a list under the block. | Not needed. This resolves the finding. |

## What would change my mind

Findings 1 and 2 are the same fault seen from two sides: the radar is a good reader with a weak right hand. If the queue can promote and the profile can record what I have learned, this becomes an instrument I would defend to a colleague.
