# P5 - UX and accessibility researcher (round 2, on the R1 build)

## Verdict

Both blocking findings are repaired. **The item rows now carry `role="button"`, `tabindex="0"`, `aria-pressed`, and a keydown handler for Enter and Space, and 160 of 160 rows are focusable** — T09 now passes at every step in the page model. **The radar is published at https://fboschman.github.io/ReadingRadar/, so T10 starts and the Monday review fits a phone.** The count of a filtered list is announced with `aria-live="polite"`, there is an explicit focus ring, and the triage buttons are real buttons. Three smaller things remain: the selected row is still marked by a shadow as well as by `aria-pressed`, the abstract is a long block with no disclosure on a narrow screen, and the queue's mark buttons repeat three times per row, which is a lot of tab stops for a 200-row list.

## Findings

| # | Sev | Feature | Finding | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S2 | F08 | The selected state is carried by a background shadow and by `aria-pressed`. The visual mark is weaker than the semantic one. | `renderItemList` adds `sel` and `aria-pressed`. Needs a human eye on the contrast. | A left border and a bolder title for the selected row. |
| 2 | S3 | F09 | A long abstract in a two-column grid is a heavy block on a phone. | No disclosure control. | Put the abstract behind a "show the abstract" disclosure under 700 pixels. |
| 3 | S3 | F23 | Each queue row holds three buttons plus a link, so a keyboard user needs four tab stops per row and 800 for the list. | `renderQueue` renders three marks and one source link per candidate. | One mark control per row with a small menu, or keys (k, m, s) with a visible hint. |

## What would change my mind

Nothing blocks me. I would accept the page as usable by keyboard and on a phone today. The three findings are refinements, and I would let the owner's own session decide whether finding 1 needs the stronger mark.
